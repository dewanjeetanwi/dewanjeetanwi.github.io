About Me

I hold a B.S.S. (Honours) and an M.S.S. in Economics from the University of Chittagong, Bangladesh, where the medium of instruction for both degrees was English. My training is in agricultural economics, resource and environmental economics, health economics, and econometrics.

From 2018 to 2025 I taught mathematics and science at Bangladesh Elementary School in Chattogram, Bangladesh, working in three of the school's four units. Teaching young children showed me how people understand the world when ideas are drawn, mapped, and connected to daily life.

I now live in Blacksburg, Virginia, and I am preparing to begin a master's degree in Fall 2027. My questions are about coastal communities: how families earn, eat, and adapt as their land and water change.

Research

Working paper, 2026

Do Coastal Households Eat Differently? Fish, Diet Diversity and Economic Status in Coastal and Inland Rural Bangladesh, 2011/12–2018/19

Compares fish consumption and diet diversity between coastal and inland rural households across three rounds of the Bangladesh Integrated Household Survey, using a panel of 4,603 households, the official list of 19 coastal districts, and distance to the coast.
